\begin{abstract}
Missing trait values are a pervasive obstacle in comparative biology and macroecology, motivating a rich literature on phylogenetic imputation. This review benchmarks the principal families of methods: Bayesian phylogenetic mixed models, multivariate regression trees, missForest with phylogenetic predictors, and Rphylopars-style variance-covariance approaches. Using published simulation criteria — error magnitude, bias, coverage of credible intervals, and sensitivity to phylogenetic uncertainty — we compare performance across trait types (continuous vs. categorical), missingness regimes, and tree sizes. We distill practical guidance: report imputation uncertainty in downstream analyses, prefer multivariate over univariate imputation when correlated traits exist, and validate on held-out taxa rather than random entries.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} phylogenetic imputation, trait evolution, comparative methods, missing data.
\end{abstract}
